Lesson Plan: Real-World Signed Number Problems

Topic: Multi-Step Word Problems Involving Temperature Shifts, Bank Balances, and Elevation Changes (ILAW Framework)

I – ILLUMINATE & INTRODUCE

1. Learning Objectives

  • Cognitive: Formulate and solve multi-step word problems involving temperature shifts, bank balances, and elevation changes using signed numbers.
  • Affective: Appreciate the practical utility of mathematics in making informed financial and environmental decisions in everyday life.
  • Psychomotor: Construct a clear, chronological math model (equation translation) from a complex word problem text.

2. Initiating Hook

Ask students: "When running a community savings project or tracking weather shifts during a mountain climb, how do positive and negative numbers help us keep track of sequential increases, drops, credits, and debits without losing our place?"

L – LEARN

1. Strategy for Multi-Step Word Problems

Real-world problems rarely happen in a single operation. To tackle multi-step problems successfully, follow a structured framework:

  1. Identify Key Terms: Look for directional keywords (e.g., above/below zero, deposit/withdraw, rise/drop, ascent/descent).
  2. Translate into Expressions: Write out the sequence of operations step-by-step using correct signs.
  3. Execute Following Order of Operations (PEMDAS/GMDAS): Perform calculations carefully, keeping track of sign rules for addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.
  4. Evaluate Context: Check if the final numerical sign and units make sense within the real-world scenario.

2. Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1 (Bank Balance & Micro-Business Ledger):

A small cooperative savings account starts the week with a balance of +₱1,250. On Tuesday, a member makes a withdrawal of ₱450. On Thursday, an emergency operational fee charge of ₱300 is debited. Finally, on Friday, a cooperative dividend bonus equal to twice the current remaining balance is deposited. What is the final ending balance?

  1. Start: +1250
  2. Withdrawal on Tuesday: 1250 - 450 = 800
  3. Fee on Thursday: 800 - 300 = 500
  4. Deposit on Friday (twice the balance): 500 + 2(500) = 500 + 1000 = 1500

Result: Final balance is +₱1,500

Example 2 (Elevation & Temperature Shift):

A weather research team starts at a base camp located at an elevation of +300 meters where the temperature is +24°C. They hike up to a ridge at +1,200 meters. For every 200-meter increase in elevation, the temperature drops by 1.5°C. What is the temperature at the ridge?

  1. Calculate total elevation change: 1200 - 300 = 900 meters ascent.
  2. Determine number of 200-meter increments: 900 ÷ 200 = 4.5 increments.
  3. Calculate total temperature drop: 4.5 × (-1.5°C) = -6.75°C.
  4. Apply drop to starting temperature: 24 + (-6.75) = 17.25°C.

Result: The temperature at the ridge is +17.25°C

A – APPLY

Guided Real-World Application Task

Community Agricultural Store Inventory & Water Supply:

A local agricultural supply storage facility maintains a water reservoir for crop irrigation. The reservoir starts with a water volume level recorded at +500 liters above baseline.

  • Day 1: Irrigation pumps release 180 liters (-180).
  • Day 2: Heavy rainfall adds 250 liters (+250).
  • Day 3: Evaporation and scheduled distribution lead to a loss of 3 times 70 liters (3 × -70 = -210).

Task: Construct a multi-step signed expression and evaluate the final water volume relative to baseline.

Model: 500 - 180 + 250 - (3 × 70) = 570 - 210 = +360 liters.

W – WIND-UP

1. Formative Self-Check Quiz

  1. Problem: An airplane cruising at an altitude of +8,500 feet descends 3,200 feet, encounters turbulence forcing a sudden drop of 800 feet, and then climbs 1,500 feet. What is the final aircraft altitude?
    Answer: +6,000 feet (8500 - 3200 - 800 + 1500 = 6000)
  2. Problem: A frozen food storage locker starts at -4°C. Due to a power fluctuation, the temperature rises by 11°C over two hours, and then backup generators kick in, cooling it down by 7°C. What is the final temperature?
    Answer: 0°C (-4 + 11 - 7 = 0)

2. Synthesis & Key Takeaways

  • Sign Consistency: Always assign positive values to increases/deposits/ascents and negative values to decreases/withdrawals/descents.
  • Sequential Precision: Compute multi-step problems in order, applying PEMDAS/GMDAS rules accurately at every transition.
