ILAW Framework Grade 11 Mathematics Setting Up Equations

Lesson Plan: Identifying Variables, Constants, and Total Values

Curriculum Framework: K-12 Curriculum (60 Minutes)

Subject / Level: Grade 11 Mathematics / Algebra
Time Frame: 60 Minutes (1 Session)
Learning Model: ILAW (Introduce, Learn, Apply, Wind-up)

I. Learning Objectives

  • Cognitive: Identify variables, coefficients (unit rates), constants (fixed amounts), and total values in short word problems and set up the corresponding linear equation with 85% accuracy.
  • Affective: Demonstrate active participation and persistence during collaborative group discussions on real-world financial and daily scenarios.
  • Psychomotor: Construct and complete structured component breakdown charts and equations from given contextual word problems.

II. Learning Procedure (ILAW Framework)

I — INTRODUCE (10 Minutes)

Hook & Discussion Starter: Present a simple real-world mystery on the board:

"Juan takes a tricycle to school. The driver charges a minimum fare of ₱15 just for getting on, plus ₱2.50 for every kilometer traveled. If Juan traveled 4 kilometers, how much did he pay?"

Guided Discussion: Ask the students to break down the total cost into three distinct questions:

  • "Which part of the fare NEVER changes?" → ₱15 base charge (Constant).
  • "Which part CHANGES depending on the distance?" → ₱2.50 per kilometer (Unit Rate × Variable).
  • "What is the final result?" → Total fare paid (Total Value).

Objective Announcement: Explain that today's goal is to master setting up equations by identifying variables, constants, and total values in short word problems using y = mx + b.

L — LEARN (20 Minutes)

1. Direct Instruction: Mapping the Linear Equation Framework

Introduce the master blueprint for setting up real-life linear equations:

Total Value = (Unit Rate × Variable) + Fixed Constant
y = mx + b

2. Defining Component Roles:

  • Variable (x): The unknown quantity that changes (e.g., number of hours, distance in km, items bought).
  • Coefficient (m): The unit rate attached to the variable. Signal words: "per", "each", "every", "rate of".
  • Constant (b): The fixed starting amount that does not change. Signal words: "fixed fee", "initial amount", "base charge", "deposit".
  • Total Value (y): The final outcome or total amount accumulated.

3. Modeling Addition vs. Subtraction Scenarios:

  • Earning / Accumulating (+): E = 50h + 100 (Base allowance + hourly pay)
  • Spending / Decreasing (−): B = 500 − 45x (Initial budget − total cost of items)
A — APPLY (20 Minutes)

Collaborative Group Activity: "Equation Designers" (12 Mins)

Divide the class into 4 groups. Provide each group with a scenario card and ask them to fill out a breakdown matrix on a mini poster board:

  • Group 1 (Allowance): A student gets a ₱200 weekly allowance and earns ₱30 for each chore completed (c). Write an equation for total money (M).
  • Group 2 (Delivery Service): A bakery charges a flat delivery fee of ₱50 plus ₱12 per box of bread (x). Write an equation for total cost (A).
  • Group 3 (Remaining Load/Data): A prepaid promo starts with 1,000 MB of data and uses 50 MB for every hour of video streaming (h). Write an equation for remaining data (D).
  • Group 4 (Overtime Pay): An employee has a daily wage of ₱500 and receives ₱80 for every overtime hour (h). Write an equation for total earnings (W).

Poster Presentation (8 Mins): Each group presents their identified components (Variable, Coefficient, Constant, Total) and their final equation in 1 minute.

W — WIND-UP (10 Minutes)

1. Key Takeaway & Synthesis (2 Mins): Reiterate that whenever students read a word problem, finding keywords like "per" indicates the coefficient, while words like "fixed" or "initial" point to the constant.

2. Formative Exit Ticket (5 Mins): Students answer individually on a 1/4 sheet of paper:

"A gym membership costs an initial registration fee of ₱300 plus ₱150 per month (m). Identify the Variable, Coefficient, Constant, and write the equation for Total Cost (C)."

3. Homework / Extension (3 Mins): Distribute the Discussion Sheet: Setting Up Equations for students to review and complete the reflection questions in their notebooks.
