Teacher Discussion Guide

Topic 4.2: Translating Verbal Phrases

Handling order-sensitive phrasing and multi-operation expressions

Discussion Objective: Train students to recognize order-sensitive subtraction/division phrases (e.g., "5 less than a number" → x − 5) and systematically parse multi-operation phrases using grouping symbols.

1. Handling Order-Sensitive Phrasing

In addition and multiplication, order does not alter the result due to the commutative property. However, subtraction and division are order-sensitive.

Teacher Prompt: "If you have 10 apples and I take 3 away, do you have the same amount as if you had 3 apples and I took 10 away? No! Order matters in subtraction. When translating words to math, we must watch out for 'turn-around' phrases."

Direct Order Phrases

The terms stay in the exact sequence as stated in English.

  • "10 decreased by x" → 10 − x
  • "The difference of 10 and x" → 10 − x
  • "10 subtracted by x" → 10 − x

"Turn-Around" Phrases (Order-Sensitive)

Flip the order of terms! The starting amount comes second.

  • "5 less than a number x" → x − 5
  • "5 subtracted from x" → x − 5
  • "5 reduced from x" → x − 5

2. Navigating Multi-Operation Phrases

When phrases contain multiple operational signals, students must identify whether operations apply to individual terms or grouped quantities.

Teacher Prompt: "Look for 'container words' like sum, difference, or quantity. These signal that an entire operation must be completed first inside parentheses before applying the next operation."
Verbal Phrase Mathematical Expression Key Breakdown
"3 times a number x, increased by 7" 3x + 7 Multiplication applies only to x; then add 7.
"3 times the sum of a number x and 7" 3(x + 7) "Sum of" groups x and 7 together inside parentheses first.
"4 less than twice a number n" 2n − 4 Combines "twice" (2n) and "less than" (swaps 4 to the end).
"The quotient of 5 and the difference of y and 2" 5 / (y − 2) "Difference of" places (y − 2) together in the denominator.

3. Essential Takeaways for Students

  • Circle the Action Words: Always identify the operation keywords first before writing any numbers or variables.
  • Watch for "Than" and "From": Phrases ending in "than" or "from" require flipping the order of terms.
  • Parentheses Protect Groups: Words like "the sum of..." or "the difference of..." demand parentheses around that operation before performing subsequent operations.
