ILAW Framework Grade 11 Mathematics Topic 4.3

Lesson Plan: Translating Symbolic Equations into Real-Life Contexts

Curriculum Framework: K-12 Curriculum (60 Minutes)

Subject / Level: Grade 11 Mathematics / Algebra
Time Frame: 60 Minutes (1 Session)
Learning Model: ILAW (Introduce, Learn, Apply, Wind-up)

I. Learning Objectives

  • Cognitive: Translate symbolic equations (e.g., y = mx + b) into meaningful, real-life contextual statements with 85% accuracy.
  • Affective: Demonstrate appreciation for the practical utility of algebra by engaging actively in group discussions on local economic scenarios (e.g., Suki discounts, local transport fares).
  • Psychomotor: Construct and present a real-life contextual scenario based on a given linear equation using a visual diagram or graphic organizer.

II. Learning Procedure (ILAW Framework)

I — INTRODUCE (10 Minutes)

Hook & Context Setting: Write C = 15 + 2k prominently on the board without initial context.

Teacher Script: "Class, imagine you take a local tricycle every morning. Looking at this mathematical formula, can anyone guess what the number 15 and the number 2 might represent in terms of your daily fare?"

Interactive Prompt: Allow students 2 minutes to talk with a partner. Elicit responses like "15 is the minimum flag-down fare" and "2 is the extra charge per kilometer."

Objective Announcement: Explicitly state that today's goal is to turn symbolic mathematical equations into meaningful real-world stories and statements.

L — LEARN (20 Minutes)

1. Deconstructing the Equation: Direct instruction on how each part of an equation mapping to real life works:

  • Variable (k): Unknown quantity or input (e.g., distance traveled in kilometers).
  • Coefficient (2): The rate per unit (e.g., ₱2.00 per kilometer).
  • Constant (15): The fixed starting amount or base charge (e.g., ₱15 base fare).
  • Output (C): The total outcome (e.g., total fare in pesos).

2. Standardizing the Translation Steps:

  1. Step 1: Define the variables clearly (e.g., "Let p = purchase amount").
  2. Step 2: Identify operations as real-world actions (+ is added fee, − is discount/deduction, × is rate per item).
  3. Step 3: Translate = using active phrases like "results in" or "amounts to".

3. Guided Practice: Work through P = 0.90s − 150 as a class (Net profit after a 10% suki discount minus a ₱150 daily stall rental fee).

A — APPLY (20 Minutes)

Collaborative Group Activity (12 Mins): Divide the class into 4 small groups. Hand each group a card with an equation:

  • Group 1: E = 50h + 100 (Overtime pay / allowance scenario)
  • Group 2: T = 500 − 45x (Purchasing / remaining budget scenario)
  • Group 3: S = 0.50p − 20 (Store discount scenario)
  • Group 4: A = 12x + 50 (Bakery packaging fee / unit rate scenario)

Task: Create a short 2-sentence contextual story for the equation, clearly stating variable definitions, rates, and fixed values on a mini poster board.

Group Presentations (8 Mins): Each group presents their contextual scenario in 1–2 minutes.

W — WIND-UP (10 Minutes)

1. Synthesis & Key Takeaway (2 Mins): Reiterate that algebra is simply written shorthand for real-world business, transportation, and personal budgeting rules.

2. Individual Formative Evaluation (5 Mins): On a 1/2 sheet of paper, translate the following:

  1. W = 80h + 500 (Where W = total wage in pesos, h = overtime hours worked)
  2. B = 1000 − 120n (Where B = remaining budget, n = number of school supplies bought)

3. Homework Assignment (3 Mins): Ask students to observe one financial or transport transaction at home or in their neighborhood (e.g., sari-sari store purchase, electric bill) and construct a 3-sentence contextual story with its corresponding symbolic equation.
