Detailed Lesson Plan in Mathematics

Topic 4.2: Translating Verbal Phrases into Algebraic Expressions

Subject: General Mathematics / Algebra
Curriculum: K-12 Curriculum
Time Allotment: 60 Minutes
Grade Level: Grade 11

I. Learning Objectives

At the end of the 60-minute lesson, the students should be able to:

  • Cognitive Differentiate direct-order from order-sensitive ("turn-around") verbal phrases and translate multi-operation statements into algebraic expressions correctly.
  • Affective Value precision and careful reading when converting verbal statements into mathematical symbols, recognizing its importance in daily transactions and fair trade (Suki discount system).
  • Psychomotor Formulate algebraic expressions corresponding to verbal phrases representing localized real-life scenarios.

II. Subject Matter

  • Topic: Translating Verbal Phrases into Algebraic Expressions (Order-Sensitive & Multi-Operation Phrases)
  • Reference: K-12 Curriculum Guide for Senior High School Mathematics / Grade 11 General Mathematics
  • Key Concepts:
    • Order-Sensitive Phrases: Phrases requiring a reversal of terms ("less than", "subtracted from").
    • Grouping Signals: Words acting as containers (sum, difference, quantity) requiring parentheses.
  • Instructional Materials: Word phrase flashcards, localized scenario task sheets, whiteboard markers.

III. Instructional Procedure (ILAW)

I
INTRODUCE
10 Minutes

Contextual Hook: The Suki Discount Dilemma

"Aling Nena runs a local sari-sari store. To help her loyal suki (regular customer), she offers a discount of ₱5 off the total bill x. Should Aling Nena write this transaction mathematically as 5 − x or x − 5? Why does the order matter?"

Solicit answers from students to highlight that order alters the financial result. Introduce Topic 4.2: Mastering order-sensitive phrases (where sequence matters) and multi-operation expressions (where grouping symbols are needed).

L
LEARN
20 Minutes

1. Direct Mapping vs. Order-Sensitive ("Turn-Around") Phrases
Explain that addition and multiplication are commutative, but subtraction and division are non-commutative and order-sensitive.

Verbal Phrase Type Algebraic Expression Explanation
"10 decreased by x" Direct Order 10 − x Follows exact reading order.
"10 less than x" Turn-Around x − 10 "Less than" shifts 10 to the end.
"y subtracted by 8" Direct Order y − 8 Direct subtraction order.
"y subtracted from 8" Turn-Around 8 − y Start with 8 and take y away.
Teacher Anchor Rule: "Whenever you spot trigger phrases ending in 'than' or 'from' (e.g., less than, subtracted from), flip the order of the terms!"

2. Parsing Multi-Operation Phrases & Grouping Symbols
Demonstrate how to identify "container words" (sum, difference) that group operations inside parentheses:

  • Example A (Without Grouping): "4 times a number n, increased by 6" → 4n + 6
  • Example B (With Grouping): "4 times the sum of a number n and 6" → 4(n + 6)
A
APPLY
20 Minutes

Group Activity: Bayanihan Translation Challenge

Divide the class into small groups. Provide each group with an activity board containing localized situational statements to translate into mathematical expressions.

Task 1: Market & Transport Scenarios
  • "A tricycle driver charges a base fare of ₱15 plus ₱2 per kilometer k." → 15 + 2k
  • "A vendor’s daily profit p is ₱200 less than thrice his total sales s." → 3s − 200
  • "Half the total weight of a kilograms of rice and b kilograms of corn." → (a + b) / 2
Task 2: Spot the Mistake! (Error Analysis)

Each group analyzes the statement: "5 less than twice a number x is written as 5 − 2x."
Group Deliverable: Identify why it is incorrect and write the correct translation (2x − 5).

W
WRAP-UP
10 Minutes

1. Synthesis & Reflection:

  • What trigger words flip the order of subtraction? ("less than", "subtracted from")
  • How do we know when to use parentheses? (Look for container words like "sum of" or "difference of").

2. Formative Assessment / Exit Ticket:

Direct students to translate the following 3 items on a short slip of paper before leaving:

  1. 12 subtracted from a number y
  2. Twice the difference of m and 7
  3. 8 less than 5 times a number n
Teacher Reference & Exit Ticket Answer Key
  1. "12 subtracted from a number y" → y − 12
  2. "Twice the difference of m and 7" → 2(m − 7)
  3. "8 less than 5 times a number n" → 5n − 8
