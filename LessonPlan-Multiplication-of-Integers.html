Lesson Plan: Multiplication & Division of Signed Numbers

Topic 1.4: Rules for Same Signs, Different Signs, and Repeated Multiplication / Powers of Signed Numbers (ILAW Framework)

I – ILLUMINATE & INTRODUCE

1. Learning Objectives

  • Cognitive: Determine the product and quotient of signed numbers using core sign rules, and evaluate repeated multiplication expressions.
  • Affective: Value accuracy and logical consistency when scaling positive and negative quantities in problem-solving.
  • Psychomotor: Write and compute multi-step signed multiplication and exponentiation problems correctly.

2. Initiating Hook

Ask students: "If a business experiences a loss of 150 pesos every single day for 5 straight days, how do we represent the total financial impact mathematically?" Lead into how multiplication scales repetitive gains or losses.

L – LEARN

1. Core Rules for Multiplication & Division

Unlike addition and subtraction, multiplication and division rely strictly on whether signs match or differ:

  • Same Signs = Positive: The product or quotient of two numbers with the same sign (Positive × Positive, or Negative × Negative) is always positive.
  • Different Signs = Negative: The product or quotient of two numbers with different signs (Positive × Negative, or Negative × Positive) is always negative.

Quick Reference Table:

  • (+) × (+) = (+)  |  (+) ÷ (+) = (+)
  • (-) × (-) = (+)  |  (-) ÷ (-) = (+)
  • (+) × (-) = (-)  |  (+) ÷ (-) = (-)
  • (-) × (+) = (-)  |  (-) ÷ (+) = (-)

2. Step-by-Step Examples

Example 1 (Multiplication - Different Signs): (-8) × 6 = ?

  1. Multiply the absolute values: 8 × 6 = 48.
  2. Check the signs: A negative times a positive results in a negative sign.

Result: (-8) × 6 = -48

Example 2 (Division - Same Signs): (-54) ÷ (-9) = ?

  1. Divide the absolute values: 54 ÷ 9 = 6.
  2. Check the signs: A negative divided by a negative results in a positive sign.

Result: (-54) ÷ (-9) = +6

3. Repeated Multiplication & Powers of Signed Numbers

When a negative number is raised to an exponent, the final sign depends on whether the exponent is even or odd:

  • Even Exponent: Results in a positive product because pairs of negative signs cancel out. (e.g., (-2)^4 = (-2) × (-2) × (-2) × (-2) = +16)
  • Odd Exponent: Results in a negative product because one negative sign remains unpaired. (e.g., (-2)^3 = (-2) × (-2) × (-2) = -8)
A – APPLY

Localized Real-World Application

Micro-Enterprise Inventory Scaling: Suppose a small bakery cooperative incurs a daily operational loss adjustment of -₱150 over a 5-day audit period.

Expression: 5 × (-150)

Application: Multiplying a positive number of days by a negative daily adjustment yields a total net impact of -₱750 (a total deficit of 750 pesos).

W – WIND-UP

1. Formative Self-Check Quiz

  1. Evaluate: (-7) × (-8)
    Answer: +56 (Same signs yield a positive product).
  2. Evaluate: (-3)^4
    Answer: +81 (Even exponent with a negative base yields a positive result).

2. Synthesis & Key Takeaways

  • Sign Alignment: Same signs produce positive results; different signs produce negative results.
  • Power Rules: Negative bases with even exponents yield positive outcomes; odd exponents retain the negative sign.
