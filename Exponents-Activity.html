Classroom Activities: Exponent Basics & Signed Bases

Interactive Activities for Topic 3.2 (ILAW Framework Alignment)

Activity 1: "Sign Sorting" Relay Game A – APPLY / Group Competition

Objective

Master the structural difference between (-x)n and -xn, as well as the impact of even vs. odd powers on signed bases.

Materials Needed

Flashcards with expressions (e.g., (-3)2, -32, (-2)3, -20, (-5)0, (-1)101), two sorting buckets labeled "POSITIVE RESULT" and "NEGATIVE RESULT".

Procedure

  1. Divide the class into 2–4 teams lined up at the back of the classroom.
  2. Place a stack of shuffled flashcards at the front of each line.
  3. On "GO!", the first student picks up a card, evaluates the final sign mentally or on scratch paper, runs to the front, and drops it into the correct bucket (Positive or Negative).
  4. The team with the most correctly categorized cards in 3 minutes wins the round.
  5. Debrief: Pull out cards that students frequently misclassified (e.g., -42 vs (-4)2) and discuss as a whole class.
Activity 2: "Pattern Detective" Discovery Grid L – LEARN / Pair Work

Objective

Derive the zero exponent rule (x0 = 1) and discover the sign alternation rule for negative bases through guided pattern completion.

Materials Needed

Printed "Pattern Detective" activity sheets.

Sample Worktable: Pattern 1 (Zero Exponent)

33 = 27  →  Divide by 3

32 = 9  →  Divide by 3

31 = 3  →  Divide by 3

30 = ?  →  (3 ÷ 3 = 1)

Sample Worktable: Pattern 2 (Negative Base Parity)

(-2)1 = -2 (1 negative factor → Negative)

(-2)2 = (-2)(-2) = +4 (2 negative factors → Positive)

(-2)3 = (-2)(-2)(-2) = -8 (3 negative factors → Negative)

(-2)4 = (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2) = +16 (4 negative factors → Positive)

Guiding Questions for Partners

  • What happens to the sign every time the exponent increases by 1 for a negative base?
  • What single rule can you summarize for any (-a)n when n is an even number versus when n is an odd number?
Activity 3: Exponent Truth & Lie Worksheet Challenge W – WIND-UP / Formative Assessment

Objective

Critique and correct common mathematical statements involving exponents and signed bases to solidify procedural accuracy.

Task: Two Truths and a Lie

For each set below, identify the LIE and write the corrected version on your whiteboard/paper.

  1. Set A:
    a) (-4)2 = 16
    b) -42 = 16
    c) (-4)0 = 1
    (Lie: b → Correct Value: -16)
  2. Set B:
    a) (-1)50 = 1
    b) (-1)51 = -1
    c) -150 = 1
    (Lie: c → Correct Value: -1)
