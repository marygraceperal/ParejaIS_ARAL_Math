Classroom Activities: Absolute Value

Targeted, interactive activities for Topic 3.1 (ILAW Framework Alignment)

Activity 1: Human Number Line (Kinesthetic) A – APPLY / Group

Objective

Physically construct a number line to experience absolute value as distance from zero, reinforcing the psychomotor learning objective.

Materials Needed

Chalk/masking tape for a line on the floor, numbered index cards from -10 to +10, and tape measures.

Procedure

  1. Mark a line on the classroom floor with 0 in the center, negative numbers to the left, and positive numbers to the right (spaced at 1-foot intervals).
  2. Assign two students to stand on specific numbers (e.g., Student A at -7, Student B at +4).
  3. Ask Student A: "How many steps are you from zero?" (Answer: 7 steps). Emphasize that steps cannot be negative (|-7| = 7).
  4. Have Student A walk to Student B and count the steps, then verify with the formula d = |-7 - 4| = |-11| = 11 steps.
Activity 2: "Distance Duel" Card Game W – WIND-UP / Pair Work

Objective

Practice calculating expression values and distance between signed numbers in a peer-supported, gamified structure.

Materials Needed

Standard deck of cards (Red cards = negative integers, Black cards = positive integers; Kings/Queens/Jacks = 10, Aces = 1).

Rules of Play

  1. Students pair up. Each student draws two cards from the shuffled deck.
  2. Round Type A (Evaluating): Each student calculates | Card 1 - Card 2 |. The player with the larger absolute result wins the round.
  3. Round Type B (Distance): Player 1 places their single card on the desk (Point A). Player 2 places theirs (Point B). Both calculate d(A, B) = |A - B| on their whiteboards. The first to compute correctly scores a point.
Activity 3: Local Elevation & Business Variance Task A – APPLY / Individual Worksheet

Objective

Apply absolute value formulas to solve real-world distance and variance word problems.

Sample Problem Set

  1. Inventory Variance: A bakery target for daily bread production is 150 units. On Monday, actual output was 138; on Tuesday, it was 162. Calculate the absolute variance | Actual - Target | for both days. What do you notice about the two variances?
    (Answer: Both equal 12 units magnitude.)
  2. Elevation Distance: A hiker starts at a station 85 meters above sea level and descends into a valley point -22 meters below sea level. Write an absolute value expression and find the total vertical distance traveled.
    (Answer: |85 - (-22)| = 107 meters.)
