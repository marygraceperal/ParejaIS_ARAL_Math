Student Study Notes Topic 4.3

Translating Expressions & Equations to Words

Verbalizing symbolic math into meaningful, real-life contextual statements

The Core Idea: Mathematics is a universal language. Translating symbolic expressions like C = 15 + 2k into real-world statements (e.g., tricycle fares) allows us to communicate real-life rules, transactions, and relationships clearly.

1. Literal Translation vs. Contextual Verbalization

There are two ways to describe a mathematical expression or equation in words:

A. Literal Word-for-Word Translation

Translates operations and variables directly without describing what the variables represent in real life.

  • 2x + 5 → "Twice a number x increased by 5."
  • 3(n − 4) = 18 → "Three times the difference of a number n and 4 is equal to 18."
B. Real-Life Contextual Verbalization

Assigns real-world meaning to variables (money, distance, items, speed) to tell a narrative or describe a system.

  • 2x + 5 → "The total cost of buying 2 notebooks at x pesos each plus a ₱5 fee."
  • 3(n − 4) = 18 → "Three times the discounted price of a shirt (reduced by ₱4) equals ₱18."

2. Deconstructing Equations into Story Elements

When verbalizing an equation, every mathematical component plays a specific role in the context:

Symbol Component Mathematical Role Real-Life Context Equivalent
Variable (x, k, h) Unknown quantity / input Kilometers traveled, hours worked, quantity of items purchased
Coefficient (2x, 50h) Rate / Multiplier Hourly wage (₱50/hr), fare rate (₱2/km), price per item
Constant (+ 15, − 100) Fixed starting point Base fare, entry fee, flat delivery charge, fixed discount
Equals Sign (=) Equality / Outcome "Results in", "amounts to", "is total to", "yields"

3. Real-Life Translation Case Studies

Case Study 1: Transport & Fare Structure

Symbolic Equation: F = 15 + 2k.5

Variable Definitions: F = Total Fare (₱), k = Distance (km)

Contextual Verbal Statement:

"The tricycle fare F consists of a base flag-down rate of ₱15 plus an additional charge of ₱2.50 for every kilometer k traveled."

Case Study 2: Daily Earnings & Suki Discounts

Symbolic Equation: P = 0.90s − 150

Variable Definitions: P = Daily Net Profit (₱), s = Gross Sales (₱)

Contextual Verbal Statement:

"A store owner's net profit P equals 90% of total sales s (after giving a 10% suki discount), minus a fixed daily stall rental fee of ₱150."

4. Summary Rules for Note Review

Student Checklist for Verbalizing Math
  • Define Variable Meaning First: Always declare what the letters represent before telling the story (e.g., Let x = number of items).
  • Identify Constants as Base Values: Treat numbers standing alone as fixed starting points, fees, or baseline values.
  • Identify Coefficients as Rates: Treat numbers attached to variables as unit rates (per kilometer, per hour, per piece).
  • Use Active Verbs for the Equals Sign: Replace = with situational phrases like "gives a total of", "amounts to", or "results in".
