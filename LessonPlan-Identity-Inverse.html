Lesson Plan: Identity & Inverse Properties

Topic 2.2: Additive/Multiplicative Identities (0 & 1) and Inverses (Zero Pairs & Reciprocals) — ILAW Framework

I – ILLUMINATE & INTRODUCE

1. Learning Objectives

  • Cognitive: Distinguish between Identity and Inverse Properties for addition and multiplication, and apply them to simplify algebraic and numerical expressions.
  • Affective: Appreciate the concept of balance and neutralization when solving equations and managing financial transactions.
  • Psychomotor: Construct zero-pair visual models and write reciprocal expressions correctly during guided exercises.

2. Initiating Hook

Ask the class: "What number can you add to a total so it remains completely unchanged? What number can you multiply by to get back to 1, or add to a debt to wipe it out to zero?" Connect these real-life balance concepts to operational identities and inverses in mathematics.

L – LEARN

1. Core Concepts & Definitions

A. Identity Properties ("Preserving Value")

  • Additive Identity (0): Adding zero to any number leaves it unchanged.
    a + 0 = a  |  Example: 15 + 0 = 15
  • Multiplicative Identity (1): Multiplying any number by one leaves it unchanged.
    a × 1 = a  |  Example: -8 × 1 = -8

B. Inverse Properties ("Neutralizing to Reach the Identity")

  • Additive Inverse (Zero Pairs): Adding a number to its opposite yields the additive identity, 0.
    a + (-a) = 0  |  Example: 7 + (-7) = 0
  • Multiplicative Inverse (Reciprocals): Multiplying a non-zero number by its reciprocal yields the multiplicative identity, 1.
    a × (1 / a) = 1 (where a ≠ 0)  |  Example: 5 × (1 / 5) = 1

⚠️ Common Pitfall: Zero in Multiplicative Inverses

Zero has NO multiplicative inverse because division by zero (1 / 0) is undefined. Also, do not confuse the Additive Identity (0) with the Multiplicative Identity (1).

2. Demonstration Problems

Example 1: Simplifying with Additive Inverses (Zero Pairs)

Evaluate: 14 + 29 + (-14) + 5

  1. Group opposites together: [14 + (-14)] + 29 + 5
  2. Apply Additive Inverse Law: 0 + 29 + 5
  3. Apply Additive Identity Law: 29 + 5 = 34

Result: 34

Example 2: Simplifying with Multiplicative Inverses (Reciprocals)

Evaluate: (3 / 7) × 12 × (7 / 3)

  1. Group reciprocals together: [(3 / 7) × (7 / 3)] × 12
  2. Apply Multiplicative Inverse Law: 1 × 12
  3. Apply Multiplicative Identity Law: 12

Result: 12

A – APPLY

Real-World Contextual Applications

Context 1: Store Cash Ledger (Additive Inverses)

A small retail store records a daily income of ₱450 and an unexpected return/refund expense of -₱450. The net effect on the account balance is ₱450 + (-₱450) = ₱0 (Zero Pair concept balancing out the transaction).

Context 2: Scaling Recipe Ingredients (Multiplicative Inverses)

If a recipe quantity is tripled (× 3) during batch production and later reduced back to a single portion by taking one-third (× 1/3), the net factor multiplier is 3 × 1/3 = 1, keeping the single-serving proportions original.

W – WIND-UP

1. Formative Assessment Check

  1. Identify Property: What property is demonstrated by -12 × (-1 / 12) = 1?
    Answer: Multiplicative Inverse Property.
  2. Solve Mentally: Simplify 8.5 + (-8.5) + 0 + 19.
    Answer: 19 (Zero pair neutralizes to 0, leaving 0 + 19 = 19).
  3. Find the Inverse: What is the additive inverse of -4/5, and what is its multiplicative inverse?
    Answer: Additive Inverse = +4/5; Multiplicative Inverse = -5/4.

2. Key Takeaways Summary

  • Identities Keep Value: Add 0 or multiply by 1 to preserve the number.
  • Inverses Neutralize: Add the opposite to reach 0; multiply by the reciprocal to reach 1.
  • Solving Utility: Identity and inverse properties are essential foundation tools for isolating variables in basic algebra.
