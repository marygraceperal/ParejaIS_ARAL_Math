Unit 1.1: Understanding Signed Numbers

1. Key Concepts & Definitions

  • Positive Numbers: Values greater than zero ($+1, +2, +5$). If no sign is written, the number is positive.
  • Negative Numbers: Values less than zero ($-1, -2, -5$). Always written with a negative sign ($-$).
  • Zero ($0$): The neutral origin point on a number line. It is neither positive nor negative.

2. Real-World Applications

Context Positive Representation (+) Negative Representation (-)
Money & Finance Earning or depositing (e.g., $+₱500$) Debt or spending (e.g., $-₱500$)
Elevation Above sea level (e.g., $+100\text{m}$) Below sea level (e.g., $-50\text{m}$)
Temperature Above $0^\circ\text{C}$ (e.g., $+32^\circ\text{C}$) Below $0^\circ\text{C}$ (e.g., $-5^\circ\text{C}$)

3. Comparing & Ordering Signed Numbers

Golden Rule: On a horizontal number line, the number further to the RIGHT is always GREATER.

  • Any positive number is greater than zero and any negative number ($5 > -100$).
  • Zero is greater than any negative number ($0 > -8$).
  • When comparing two negative numbers, the one closer to zero is greater ($-3 > -9$).

4. Common Misconception Alert

⚠️ Watch Out!
Students often think $-10$ is larger than $-2$ because $10$ is larger than $2$.
Correction: Think of debt! Having a debt of $₱2$ ($-2$) is better/higher than having a debt of $₱10$ ($-10$). Therefore, $-2 > -10$.
