Learning Objective: Accurately translate order-sensitive verbal phrases (e.g., "less than", "subtracted from") and multi-operation statements into algebraic expressions with correct grouping symbols.

Part I: Direct Order vs. "Turn-Around" Subtraction

Translate each verbal phrase into an algebraic expression. Watch out for phrases that flip the sequence!

  1. 10 decreased by a number x →
  2. 10 less than a number x →
  3. A number y subtracted by 14 →
  4. A number y subtracted from 14 →
  5. 6 reduced by twice a number m →
  6. 6 reduced from twice a number m →

Part II: Multi-Operation & Grouping Challenge

Translate the following phrases using parentheses where container words (like sum or difference) appear.

  1. 5 times a number n, increased by 8 →
  2. 5 times the sum of a number n and 8 →
  3. The difference of thrice a number p and 4 →
  4. Thrice the difference of a number p and 4 →
  5. The quotient of 12 and the sum of a and 3 →

Teacher Answer Key

Part I: Direct Order vs. "Turn-Around"

  1. 10 − x
  2. x − 10
  3. y − 14
  4. 14 − y
  5. 6 − 2m
  6. 2m − 6

Part II: Multi-Operation & Grouping Challenge

  1. 5n + 8
  2. 5(n + 8)
  3. 3p − 4
  4. 3(p − 4)
  5. 12 / (a + 3)  or  ½ fraction form

Part III: Spot the Error!

  1. Incorrect. Correction: 2k − 8.
    Reason: "Less than" requires flipping the terms so 8 is subtracted at the end.
  2. Incorrect. Correction: 3(x − 7).
    Reason: "The quantity" indicates that (x − 7) must be enclosed in parentheses before multiplying by 3.
