Lesson Plan: Exponent Basics & Signed Bases

Topic 3.2: Expanded Form, Zero Exponents (x⁰ = 1), and Even vs. Odd Signed Powers (ILAW Framework)

I – ILLUMINATE & INTRODUCE

1. Learning Objectives

  • Cognitive: Formulate rules for evaluating expressions involving zero exponents (x0 = 1) and negative bases raised to even or odd powers.
  • Affective: Appreciate the precision required when using grouping symbols (parentheses) to avoid common algebraic errors in real-world calculations.
  • Psychomotor: Expand exponential terms into repeated multiplication factors and compute evaluated expressions accurately.

2. Initiating Hook

Write on the board: (-2)4 and -24. Ask the class: "Are these two expressions identical, or will they give different results?" Conduct a quick hand vote before revealing that one equals +16 while the other equals -16. Explain that understanding how signs interact with exponents prevents costly errors in mathematics and science.

L – LEARN

1. Exponential Rules & Mathematical Foundations

Definition of Exponent: bn = b × b × ... × b (n factors of base b).

Zero Exponent Rule: For any non-zero real number x ≠ 0, x0 = 1.

2. Signed Bases: Parity Rule

Condition Rule Example Result
Negative Base, Even Exponent (-a)even = +an (-3)2 = (-3)(-3) +9
Negative Base, Odd Exponent (-a)odd = -an (-3)3 = (-3)(-3)(-3) -27

3. Worked Examples

a) Evaluate (-5)3 = (-5) × (-5) × (-5) = -125

b) Evaluate 70 + (-12)0 = 1 + 1 = 2

c) Evaluate (-1)42 - (-1)17 = 1 - (-1) = 1 + 1 = 2

⚠️ Common Diagnostic Misconception: The Negation Distinction

Remind students that (-x)n includes the negative sign in repeated multiplication, whereas -xn applies the exponent ONLY to x, making the outer negative sign apply last.
Example: -42 = -(4 × 4) = -16, but (-4)2 = (-4) × (-4) = +16.

A – APPLY

Contextualized Real-World Applications

Application 1: Population and Cell Growth Modeling

Bacterial cultures double every hour following P(t) = P0 × 2t. At time t = 0 hours (initial start), P(0) = P0 × 20 = P0 × 1 = P0. The zero exponent rule guarantees the initial count remains unchanged before time elapses.

Application 2: Financial Compounding & Accounting Adjustment Factors

In financial formulas using growth factor expressions like (1 + r)t, if the period compounding factor is zero (t = 0), (1 + r)0 = 1, representing the principal amount prior to earning interest.

W – WIND-UP

1. Formative Assessment Quiz

  1. Evaluate: (-2)5
    Answer: (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2)(-2) = -32
  2. Evaluate: -62 + (-6)2
    Answer: -36 + 36 = 0
  3. Simplify: (15x3)0 where x ≠ 0
    Answer: 1

2. Key Takeaways Summary

  • Zero Exponents: Any non-zero base raised to power 0 equals 1 (x0 = 1).
  • Even vs. Odd Powers: Negative bases inside parentheses become positive with even powers and stay negative with odd powers.
  • Order of Operations: Parentheses dictate whether a negative sign is part of the base or an operation performed after exponentiation.
